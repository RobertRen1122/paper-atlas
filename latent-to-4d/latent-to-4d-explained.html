Latent-to-4D, Explained

“Beyond Pixels”: skip the RGB round trip — resample a Wan video latent onto 4RC’s token grid, run the frozen 4D hierarchy with rank-16 LoRA, and read out cameras and world-space point maps for all 81 frames

Every text-to-4D or image-to-4D system has to get from a video prior to explicit geometry. The usual route is generate-then-reconstruct: sample a video, decode it to pixels, hand the pixels to a feed-forward 4D reconstructor. Latent-to-4D (Zhejiang University ReLER, arXiv 2608.10744) argues that the pixels are an unnecessary detour. Every Wan DiT that decodes through the same VAE ends its sampler with a latent in the same 16-channel space, so one geometry-supervised pathway trained on encoded real videos can be pointed at any of those DiTs’ generated latents. The pathway itself — L4AR — is deliberately thin: a fixed trilinear resample, one learned 3D convolution, the frozen 4RC transformer hierarchy with rank-16 LoRA, and 4RC’s depth/ray/camera heads re-trained on ~1.1K reconstruction clips.

How this page is grounded. The authors’ repository (hayd-zju/Beyond-Pixels @ 5975c3e) is a README: inference code, training code and weights are all listed as “coming soon”, and the arXiv v1 PDF (9 pages) refers to an appendix it does not contain. So the L4AR-specific facts here come from the paper text and project page, while every tensor shape is taken from the two open components the paper says it freezes and reuses: 4RC @ a08ca0a (the “refinement hierarchy” and “prediction heads”; its released checkpoint has 1,520.07 M parameters, which I reproduce exactly by instantiating the model) and Wan2.1 @ 9737cba (the VAE and the DiTs). Anything the paper does not state — the video resolution, the aligned grid size M, which 31 of 4RC’s 40 blocks are kept, the LoRA target set — is marked ◇ and argued for, not asserted; §9 collects all of them.

1The pipeline: one pathway, two latent sources

The whole design is the observation that z_obs = μ(E_v(V)) (a real video pushed through the frozen Wan VAE encoder) and z_gen = G_θ(c, ε) (the last denoised latent of a Wan DiT) live in the same tensor space: same checkpoint, same per-channel normalisation, same [16, T', H/8, W/8] layout. Training uses only the first kind, because that is where 4D annotations exist; inference swaps in the second and changes nothing downstream. For 81 frames at 480×832 (Wan’s default frame count, the 1.3B model’s only resolution) the latent is 16×21×60×104: (81−1)/4+1 = 21 latent frames, 480/8×832/8 = 60×104.

2Cascade vs. lift: what the RGB round trip costs

The controlled comparison in the paper holds the generated latent fixed and only changes what sits between it and 4RC’s transformer. In the cascade that is a 3D VAE decoder, 81×3×480×832 pixels, 4RC’s 14×14 patch embedding and its RGB-trained early blocks. In the lift it is a resample and one convolution. Everything from the alternating-attention hierarchy onward is the same 4RC network — frozen in the cascade, frozen-plus-LoRA in the lift.

3Alignment: a resample and one convolution

The two representations disagree on all three axes: the latent has 21 frames, the 4D hierarchy wants one token row per output frame (81, per the project page); the latent grid is H/8×W/8, the hierarchy’s grid is whatever 4RC was trained on; the latent has 16 channels, the tokens 1536. The paper resolves time and space with a fixed trilinear resample and channels with a learned 3D convolution, in that order (Eq. 5: Q(0) = Flatten(S_φ(R(z)))). The convolution is the only trainable module before the hierarchy, and it is small — 0.67 M parameters if the kernel is 3×3×3, 26 K if it is 1×1×1 ◇.

4The refinement hierarchy: where tokens enter, where global attention starts, where the decoder reads

4RC’s transformer is a DINOv2 ViT-g/14 with the VGGT-style alternating-attention recipe bolted on from block 13: the first thirteen blocks see one frame at a time, then blocks 13…39 alternate global (all frames, one sequence) and frame-wise attention, and four of the global blocks are tapped for the decoder. Latent-to-4D keeps 31 of those blocks frozen and adds rank-16 LoRA. The strip below shows the schedule that the 4RC code actually runs, with the entry point the paper’s description implies.

5The block: DINOv2 ViT-g with LoRA on the side

Inside every one of the 31 blocks the computation is the stock DINOv2 giant block — pre-LayerNorm, 24-head attention, LayerScale, residual, pre-LayerNorm, SwiGLU, LayerScale, residual — with two additions 4RC made for multi-view use (per-head qk-norm and 2D RoPE from block 13) and one Latent-to-4D makes (rank-16 LoRA, “lightweight trainable updates Δψ”). The frame-wise/global distinction lives entirely in how the token tensor is reshaped before the block: the weights are identical, only the sequence over which attention runs changes.

6The 4D heads: depth and rays from a dual DPT, cameras from a token

The “4D Decoder” of the paper is 4RC’s head set with the tracking parts switched off. The four tapped 3072-d feature maps go through one DPT projection/resize stage and then two separate RefineNet fusion chains: the main chain ends in depth (+ confidence) at the pixel grid, the auxiliary chain ends in a 6-channel world ray (+ confidence) at four pyramid levels. The camera token from the last tap goes through a small MLP into nine numbers. The paper’s Eq. 6 then composes them: P_t(u) = o_t + d_t(u)·r_t(u).

7The compatibility boundary: who shares the interface

“Compatible” is defined operationally: same VAE checkpoint, same latent normalisation, same tensor layout, same compression convention, and a latent shape L4AR has seen. Everything else about the generator — DiT width, MoE experts, conditioning, control adapters — may differ, which is what lets one L4AR checkpoint sit behind text-to-video, image-to-video, and (project page) audio-, pose-, trajectory- and action-conditioned Wan variants. The boundary is checkable in the Wan configs.

8Worth knowing from the paper (arXiv 2608.10744)

  • Training is small and staged. The report says the final stage uses 1,143 clips from six reconstruction datasets (“roughly 1K existing reconstruction clips”), that training is multi-stage with the trainable pieces “progressively activated to avoid destabilizing the pretrained geometric prior”, and that it starts “from a 4RC-aligned latent adapter” — the most natural reading is a first stage that fits the alignment module to 4RC’s own token space before geometry supervision, but the sentence is all the paper says. The project page adds the scale: 81-frame clips, “≈1K clips · ≈1 h”, and a “300-step final refinement on 4 GPUs”. Dataset names, LoRA placement and per-stage budgets are deferred to an appendix absent from arXiv v1.
  • Three loss groups on metric 4D annotations. Lunc: confidence-weighted depth, depth-gradient and world-ray terms; Lcam: translation, rotation and FoV; Lgeom: metric depth and ray supervision plus mean and tail errors on world-space points and a surface-normal loss. Nothing is supervised in RGB space and no distillation from the DiT is used — the DiT is not run in training at all.
  • The headline numbers are same-latent. On Text4D-200 the matched Wan2.1-1.3B+4RC cascade scores 54.21 DINO-F1 and Ours 57.09 (+2.88); for Wan2.1-14B, 53.56 vs 57.01 (+3.45); on I4D-200, Wan2.2-I2V-A14B+4RC 55.79 vs 61.60 (+5.81), with π³, Any4D and native 4DNeX (28.33) further behind. The one metric the paper concedes: CogVideoX-5B+4RC keeps the best RGB-reference CLIP-I (75.33 vs 72.24/72.32), “so we do not claim a uniform win on every metric”. One checkpoint gives “nearly identical performance with both text DiTs” (57.09 vs 57.01), which is the reuse claim in one number.
  • What the metrics measure. Each predicted point sequence is rendered from two off-axis cameras and scored with Text-CLIP, RGB-reference CLIP-I, DINO global similarity, valid-patch DINO matching and DINO set-F1; the paper calls these “appearance-dependent proxies for visible geometric coherence and completeness, not metric 4D accuracy”, and uses the generated RGB “only for evaluation and coloring, not as geometry input”. Fifty raters judged 50 cases per benchmark in randomised pairwise comparisons (ten ratings per case) and preferred Ours in every dimension, most for geometry and completeness (66.8% text, 72.1% image; 95% bootstrap intervals all above 50%).
  • Ground-truth ablation. On held-out 7-Scenes (18 scenes) and NRGBD (9), removing any of the four L4AR components degrades accuracy, completeness and normal consistency; the two attention scopes and the 3D convolution are the large effects (Comp 5.42→15.8–20.8 cm on 7-Scenes), the trilinear resample the small one (5.42→6.84). The paper frames this as validating the design, “rather than claiming reconstruction OOD superiority”.
  • The DiT-residual diagnostic is narrower than it sounds. The paper takes the empirical near-terminal residual z45 − z50 of a 50-step sampler, projects it onto the “Grid-Align width-null space”, adds it (scaled by ρ) to an observed-video latent, and feeds the perturbed latent to both pathways. At ρ = 0.6 point-map drift is 0.0053/0.0047 for Ours vs 0.3827/0.3160 for decode-RGB+4RC (7-Scenes/NRGBD), 30 of 30 comparisons favouring Ours. Read carefully: the perturbation is chosen in the null space of Latent-to-4D’s own Grid-Align operator, so the near-zero drift on the Ours side is guaranteed by construction; the informative half is that a component of the DiT’s last five steps which the lift cannot even see is enough to move the cascade’s geometry by tens of percent of scene radius after it passes through the VAE decoder and 4RC’s RGB encoder. The paper itself says it “isolates a residual component rather than arbitrary DiT errors”.
  • Controls come for free, correctness does not. Because nothing condition-specific is trained, the same checkpoint lifts latents from Wan-Move trajectory control, Wan-Animate, VACE pose control, Wan-S2V audio driving, video-as-prompt editing and manipulation/navigation world-model rollouts (Figs. 7–8, project page). The paper is careful that these “show interface compatibility, not action success or physical correctness”.
  • Stated limits. “Evidence remains limited to a shared VAE convention, and projection-based evaluation does not establish metric accuracy for generated scenes.” The related-work section positions the method against VIST3A (a video VAE stitched to a static 3D reconstructor, one model pair) and against feed-forward 4D generators such as 4DNeX and Diff4Splat that adapt one particular generator; Latent-to-4D’s bet is that the generator side should stay untouched.
  • What 4RC is, in one line. 4RC (Luo et al., ICML 2026, arXiv 2602.10094) is an “encode once, query anytime and anywhere” 4D reconstructor: a ViT-g video encoder plus dense depth/ray/camera heads plus a query-conditioned motion decoder for tracks and scene flow. Latent-to-4D keeps the encoder hierarchy and the dense heads and leaves the query decoder frozen and unused, so what it inherits is closer to the VGGT/π³ family than to the query interface — compare the D4RT page, where the query decoder is the model.
