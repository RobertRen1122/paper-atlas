MotionCrafter, Explained

Stable Video Diffusion’s U-Net, run once, predicting an 8-channel latent that a two-branch 4D VAE decodes into world-frame point maps and scene flow

MotionCrafter (NTU · Tencent ARC · HKUST · CUHK-SZ · Monash, arXiv 2602.08961, CVPR 2026 Highlight) asks a video generator to output geometry and motion at once: for every frame, a point map in a shared world frame plus the 3D displacement of every pixel to the next frame. The trick is not in the transformer — it is Stable Video Diffusion’s U-Net, fine-tuned end to end — but in what the U-Net is asked to predict: an 8-channel “4D latent” produced by a VAE that was not forced to match SVD’s RGB latent distribution. Everything below is traced from the release at commit 9cb4e96 (a GeometryCrafter fork over diffusers 0.31.0) and the released Hugging Face configs; the paper supplies training facts and the reasoning. The canonical run is the README’s explicit invocation, which matches the paper’s default and its timing table: --model_type determ --height 320 --width 640 --num_frames 25. Note that run.py’s bare default is the diffusion variant (§8).

1The pipeline: encode the video, run the U-Net once, decode two maps

Two SVD encoders read the clip — the VAE for per-frame latents (the U-Net’s input) and CLIP for per-frame image embeddings (the U-Net’s cross-attention context). The U-Net is evaluated a single time at a fixed timestep and its output, negated, is the 4D latent: four channels of geometry, four of motion. Two decoders of the 4D VAE turn those into a point map and a scene-flow map at full resolution, both in normalized frame-0 world coordinates.

2The representation: point maps and scene flow, both in the first camera

Following DUSt3R, the world frame is the first camera. Every frame’s point map Xi stores each pixel’s 3D position in that frame, and the scene flow Vi stores that pixel’s 3D displacement to the next frame, in the same world frame — so static background has zero flow and camera motion never enters the motion field. The normalization is the paper’s stated departure from prior geometric diffusion work: no rescaling into [−1, 1] to look like RGB, just centring and dividing by the mean distance.

3The 4D VAE: one SD-style autoencoder, cloned into a geometry branch and a motion branch

The “4D VAE” is two copies of the 2D Stable-Diffusion VAE. The geometry branch is trained first on point maps and then frozen; the motion branch is a deep copy whose decoder is widened to also read the geometry latent — the paper’s “unified fusion”, chosen over no fusion and LayerDiffuse-style offset fusion because it gave the best downstream U-Net even though it was not the best reconstruction at the VAE stage (Tab. 4). There is no temporal layer anywhere in it: it encodes and decodes frames independently.

4The U-Net block: SVD’s spatio-temporal layer, with per-frame CLIP

The backbone is diffusers’ UNetSpatioTemporalConditionModel exactly as SVD-XT ships it: four resolutions (320/640/1280/1280 channels, 5/10/20/20 heads), each layer a spatio-temporal ResNet followed by a spatio-temporal transformer. MotionCrafter’s subclass changes one thing in the forward pass: SVD broadcasts a single conditioning image’s CLIP embedding to all frames, MotionCrafter hands every frame its own. The temporal transformer, however, still takes its context from the first frame — that is baked into diffusers.

5One pass or five: deterministic vs. diffusion

The release ships two U-Nets trained on the same targets. The deterministic one takes only the video latent and predicts the 4D latent in a single evaluation; the diffusion one takes a noisy 4D latent concatenated with the video latent and is stepped by SVD’s Euler scheduler. The paper prefers the deterministic model (Tab. 7: Relp −12.4%, δp +12.7% averaged over datasets) and uses it by default; run.py’s bare default is the other one.

6Long videos: 25-frame windows, no stitching in the release

Both U-Nets are trained on 25-frame clips (the SVD-XT frame count). For longer inputs the release script processes consecutive 25-frame windows independently: it hard-codes zero overlap, and the --sliding_window flag raises NotImplementedError. Because every window is normalized to its own centroid and scale, the point maps of two windows do not share a coordinate frame — the paper’s “long monocular video” claim is, in the released code, a 25-frame claim.

7Worth knowing from the paper (arXiv 2602.08961)

  • Claim. The first video-diffusion framework to jointly reconstruct dense 4D geometry and estimate dense scene flow, feed-forward, without post-optimization; +38.64% geometry and +25.0% motion over the best prior methods averaged over the world-coordinate benchmarks (Tab. 1), and top-tier zero-shot geometry on Sintel/Monkaa/DDAD (Tab. 2).
  • The normalization finding (§3.2, Tab. 3, Fig. 4). Strict alignment of 3D values and latents with the RGB VAE “is unnecessary and can hurt”: canonical (centroid, mean-distance) normalization with the whole VAE fine-tuned beats max-normalization to [−1, 1] with a frozen or decoder-only-tuned VAE, especially on large outdoor scenes. A KL term on the latent hurt and was dropped.
  • Fusion ablation (Tab. 4). No fusion vs. offset fusion (motion latent added to the geometry latent, LayerDiffuse-style) vs. unified concatenation into the motion decoder; unified is not the best VAE reconstruction but gives the best downstream U-Net.
  • Losses. Geometry VAE: Lpoint + λdLdepth + λnLnormal with λpoint=1, λL1-D=1, λPatch-D=1, λnormal=0.2 (the depth terms project the world points with the co-normalized cameras; multimodal depth supervision improves point maps by 13.55%, App. B.1). Motion VAE: MSE on valid flow + λreg=0.01 zero-flow regularizer (“as static as possible”). U-Net, deterministic: Llatent + λGLG + λMLM, all 1.0, with the motion latent term over frames 1…N−1; diffusion: Llatent only. The gin files carry the same weights (unet_determ_unify_vae_train.gin:55).
  • Data (Tab. 8). Thirteen synthetic datasets, 1.67 M frames / 16.8 K videos; all supply geometry, only Kubric, Spring and Virtual KITTI 2 supply dense scene flow, so motion supervision is a small subset. Geometry stages sample with random stride 1–5, motion training with stride 1 (App. C, Fig. 7).
  • Schedule and compute. Geometry VAE 40 k steps, motion VAE 20 k, U-Net 40 k; AdamW 1e-4; “batch size 8 for VAE training and 25 for U-Net”; 8×40 GB GPUs, about 3 days. The gin configs use per_gpu_batch_size = 1 throughout with 8-frame VAE clips and 25-frame U-Net clips — i.e. the paper’s batch numbers count frames per GPU step (gin:29).
  • Deterministic beats denoising (App. B.3, Tab. 7). Relp 30.11→25.88 (Monkaa), 35.95→32.46 (Sintel), 24.58→21.27 (DDAD); the decoder loss adds a further 15.01% on average, 36.80% on DDAD (Tab. 6).
  • Model sizes and speed (App. C.3). U-Net 1524.62 M, video VAE encoder 34.16 M, 4D VAE decoder 99.00 M, total 1657.79 M — all three reproduced here from the released configs. 138.9 ms per frame at 25×320×640 on a 40 GB GPU, with VAE decoding (73.5 ms) costing more than the U-Net (13.4 ms).
  • Lineage. The code is a GeometryCrafter fork (same authors’ group), which is where the deterministic-at-σmax trick, the window machinery, and the unused projection flags come from; the paper positions itself against Geo4D (point maps only, no motion) and against pairwise DUSt3R-style trackers (St4RTrack, Dynamic Point Maps, Stereo4D) that need post-alignment.
